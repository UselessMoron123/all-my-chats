To numerate points on a map more "perfectly," the goal is to make the sequence **predictable** and **easy for the eye to follow**. 

Currently, your numbering is a bit "jumpy" (for example, jumping from 1 on the far right to 2 in the center, then back to 3 on the right).

Here are the three most common ways to number a map perfectly, depending on your goal:

### 1. The "Reading Order" Method (Most Intuitive)
This is the most common way for people in Western cultures to scan information.
*   **The Logic:** Start at the top-left and move across to the right. When you finish the top "row," move down to the next row and go left-to-right again.
*   **On your map:** 
    *   Number 4 or 9 would likely become #1.
    *   You would move across the top (4 -> 2 -> 1).
    *   Then move down a level (10 -> 11 -> 6 -> 3).
*   **Why it's "Perfect":** It feels natural to the eye and makes it very easy to find a specific number because you always know to look "down and to the right" for the next one.

### 2. The "S-Curve" or "Snake" Method
This is often used in logistics or archaeology because it keeps numerically close numbers physically close to each other.
*   **The Logic:** 
    *   Row 1: Left to Right.
    *   Row 2: Right to Left.
    *   Row 3: Left to Right.
*   **On your map:** You would go from 4 to 2 to 1. Instead of jumping back to the left for the next row, you would drop down and number the next set from right to left (3 -> 6 -> 5 -> 11 -> 10).
*   **Why it's "Perfect":** If a user is looking at point #12, point #11 and #13 will be right next to it, rather than on the opposite side of the map.

### 3. The "Regional/Zonal" Method
If this map represents a game world (like Kenshi, which this appears to be), it is often better to group numbers by territory.
*   **The Logic:** Divide the map into 4 or 5 big "zones" (e.g., Northwest, Northeast, Central, Southwest, Southeast). Complete all numbers in one zone before moving to the next.
*   **Why it's "Perfect":** It helps the viewer categorize the information. "Numbers 1–10 are all in the desert; numbers 11–20 are all in the swamp."

### Visual Tips for "Perfect" Presentation:

Aside from the order, you can improve the visual clarity with these steps:

1.  **Consistent Coloring:** Why is #21 blue while the rest are red? If it doesn't have a specific meaning, keep them all the same color. If it *does* mean something, include a small legend in the corner.
2.  **The "Halo" Effect:** Put a thin white or yellow border (a "stroke") around your red dots and black numbers. This prevents the numbers from getting "lost" when they are placed over dark brown or black terrain (like at #39).
3.  **Use Leader Lines for Clusters:** For areas like 31 and 32, where the points are very close, use a small line pointing to the exact spot so the numbers don't overlap the map detail.
4.  **Leading Zeros:** For a cleaner look, use "01, 02, 03..." This keeps the spacing of the numbers consistent once you get into the double digits.

**If you had to pick just one:** Go with the **Reading Order (Top-Left to Bottom-Right)**. It is the gold standard for map navigation.
