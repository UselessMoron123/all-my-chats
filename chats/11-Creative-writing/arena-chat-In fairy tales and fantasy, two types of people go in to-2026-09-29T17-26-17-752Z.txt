### **The Prophecy of the Tower**

Before Princess Serayne drew her first breath, the kingdom’s seers spoke in trembling unison: *“She will bring ruin, the kingdom shall burn by her hand.”*  

The court was silent, then erupted like shaken hornets. Some said drown the infant. Others said bind her magic. But the king, a man who loved the spectacle of mercy, declared, “Nay. Let no one say I butcher babes. She shall be spared—locked away until the end of days.”

And, thanks to the prophecy, Serayne had nothing but *time*.  

Years passed, the city below forgetting the princess who never showed her face at feasts or gardens. But in the tower, forgotten phrases stitched themselves together beneath Serayne’s fingertips. She learned to call storms, to whisper to bones, to bend the stubborn iron bars like reeds.  

And she remembered the prophecy every night. “She will bring ruin.” It was her cradle-song, her chain, her curse.  

By her thirty-third year, she realized: the prophecy hadn’t been a warning. It had been a *map*. And her parents, with their brittle fear, had marched her straight down the path of ruin.  

When stone walls finally cracked and toppled under the weight of her spell, Serayne walked free draped in lightning. The kingdom’s guards scattered before her. The heavens themselves dared not speak against her vengeance.  

The king staggered from his throne when she entered the hall. His face, once so composed, wilted like a candle before fire.  

“Daughter,” he whispered. “We tried to prevent this. You must understand—”  

Serayne’s laughter sang like iron chains snapping one by one.

The people remembered: the seers’ words had come true. Serayne, the Princess of Prophecy, had indeed brought ruin. But not because fate decreed it—because fear built the very prison that taught her to wield it.  

And once more, the tower stood empty, humming with the echo of destiny fulfilled.
